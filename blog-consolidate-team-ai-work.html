How to Consolidate Your Team's AI Work in One Place

A practical guide to turning scattered AI tools, chats, and habits into one shared way of working.

Sep 17, 2026 · 4 min read · by the Klober crew

If your team has worked with AI for a while, its AI work is probably spread thin: several tools, personal and company accounts, hundreds of conversations holding phrasing that worked, decisions made, hard-won analyses, and forgotten ideas. Most of it sits untouched, because it's hard to search and even harder to share.

Consolidating doesn't mean picking one model and banning the rest. It means agreeing on one place where the team's AI work happens, so knowledge builds up instead of scattering. This guide covers the people-and-process side, whatever platform you use.

Why Consolidate at All?

Because team knowledge is scattered. Everyone holds part of the picture in a personal chat. Working in a shared space turns private knowledge into a team asset.

Because searching one place is simpler. "What did we decide on pricing?" is easier to answer when the conversation happened where everyone can find it.

Because fewer tools are easier to secure. Every extra tool is another place holding company data, another user list, and another privacy policy.

Because context compounds. Bots that work where the team works pick up context as they go, so you re-explain less every week.

Step 1: Map Where AI Work Happens Today

First, make a simple list. Which AI tools does the team use, and for what? Which personal or company accounts exist? Who relies on AI most? Are important conversations sitting in internal tools or other chat channels? You may find the team uses more tools than you thought.

Then assign one person to coordinate. They don't need to do it all, but someone should know what's moving, what's staying, and what was decided about sensitive information.

Step 2: Pick One Home for New AI Work

Choose the shared space where the team and its bots will work from now on, ideally one where several models are available, so nobody has to leave to use the one they prefer. If you're still choosing a platform, our buyer's checklist covers what to ask.

Then set a start date. From that day, new AI work happens in the shared space by default. Don't try to move the past first; the biggest gains come from where new work happens.

Step 3: Save What Still Matters

Not every old conversation is worth keeping, but a few usually are. Ask each person to spend an hour pulling out what's still useful:

  • Decisions and their reasons. Capture them in a short background doc for each project.
  • Prompts that work. Add them to a shared prompt library instead of leaving them in personal history.
  • Key analyses and drafts. Summarize them, and save the summary somewhere shared.

A few rules while you do it:

  • Get consent. Nobody's personal chats should be shared without their agreement. Their history may include things they never meant to share.
  • Handle sensitive information. Passwords, keys, customer details. Leave them out, and rotate any key that appears in a chat; once it's been pasted anywhere, treat it as exposed.
  • Check relevance. Old experimental chats add noise. More isn't always better; noisy context leads to less focused answers.

Step 4: Set Ground Rules

A shared space needs a few shared agreements:

  • What belongs where. Which conversations happen in the shared channel, and which stay private.
  • Ownership. Each bot has a person responsible for its role, instructions, and quality.
  • Data rules. What never goes to a hosted model, and which tasks use a local model instead.

Write the rules down, keep them short, and revisit them after a month.

Step 5: Check That Context Is Building Up

After two or three weeks, test it. Ask the bots about decisions made in the shared space, like "What options did we consider for the onboarding flow?" or "What structure did we agree on for the spec?" Good answers mean the shared space is turning into memory the bots can use. If not, you may need clearer summaries, better project docs, or more of the work happening in the channel.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Running everything in parallel forever. A transition period is fine. A permanent second system means the shared space never fills up.
  • Trying to move everything at once. Start with one team or project, then expand.
  • Leaving copied notes lying around. Anything you pulled out of old chats should end up in the shared space, or be deleted.
  • Not telling the team. People should know what's changing and why.

How Long Does It Take?

It depends on the number of tools, the size of the team, and how much history matters to you. A small team can agree on a home and start working there within days, then improve gradually. Don't wait for a perfect cleanup.

Your scattered AI work isn't a lost cause; it's context waiting for a home. Map it, pick one place, save what matters, set the rules, and check that it's working, and your bots and your team will start building shared memory from the first day.
